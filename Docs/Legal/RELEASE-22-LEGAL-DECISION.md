# Legal release decision — 3DS-only candidate22

Status: **public distribution remains on hold**. This is a documented technical compliance review and decision packet, not an attorney's clearance or an Apple decision. The owner asked that all emulator, design and artwork work pause in favor of this review; those automations are paused. No third party was contacted, agreement accepted or money spent. No binary was uploaded or submitted.

## Scope and completed repairs

Candidate22 is a free US-only 3DS app based on frozen21, with original presentation and explicit local user images. The generated publisher-character collection and remote cover services are disconnected from the app and widgets. Their existing masters and public URLs are preserved and are not declared licensed or fair use. Inspected store captures use the included homebrew and original UI. Source archives and signed releases through21 remain unchanged.

Both newly built cores use OpenSSL3.5.9 under Apache2.0 instead of the bundled legacy OpenSSL/SSLeay-licensed LibreSSL implementation. Explicit static link paths, library hashes, core identifiers and platform tags are recorded. GNU's license list identifies the older OpenSSL/SSLeay combination as GPL-incompatible and distinguishes Apache2-licensed OpenSSL3+. Merely retaining notices would not have repaired that combination. The replacement addresses that specific dependency issue; it is not an App Store exception. [GNU license assessment](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html#OpenSSL), [official OpenSSL release/license information](https://openssl-library.org/source/).

The notice/source inventory now includes FAAD2's required Nero attribution, OpenSSL's full license, and the previously unlisted fallback-resource/font terms. The actual shared-font generator revision uses Montserrat4.000 (OFL1.1) and Unifont10.0.05 (GPL2-or-later with its font embedding exception). Their embedded licensing metadata, exact original input hashes, full terms and source are preserved. The independent resource generators retain their own GPL2 terms; no later-version permission is inferred from a license template or silently replaced by the EmuDen GPL3 grant. The font exception is retained without assuming it waives software-distribution requirements.

The three identified console default-data payloads are excluded by mandatory source policy, with fresh negative binary checks required for22. The package does not intentionally supply commercial games, an original Nintendo Mii archive, user saves or credentials. A user choosing a file does not establish the file's lawful origin. The actual runtime still contains entertainment-related AES/content handling and guest networking; it must not be represented as crypto-free or OS-only HTTPS.

## The unresolved Apple/GPL arrangement

The GPL grant permits copying/modification/redistribution and requires matching source under applicable conditions. GPL3 section10 restricts further conditions; section6 includes network source-delivery and a conditional Installation Information rule. Publishing a matching source link addresses source access, but is not a waiver of the other terms. Whether the software-only transaction triggers Installation Information needs assessment; this packet does not presume it automatically does. [Exact pinned GPL text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/azahar-emu/azahar/2127.0-rc1/LICENSE.txt).

Apple's Standard EULA(a) contains an open-source carve-out in its copying/modification restriction, alongside scope/redistribution limits. The public DPLA currently places FOSS compliance in3.3.4(A)(v), signing/content-protection compatibility in5.1, and minimum custom-EULA conditions in Schedule1 section3.2/ExhibitB section2. A custom EULA cannot simply be assumed to cancel Apple's minimum conditions. The precise accepted account terms and delivery/EULA arrangement have not been verified. No blanket ban on GPL apps, blanket Apple waiver, or transferable clearance from Delta/another app is asserted. [Apple Standard EULA](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/dev/stdeula/), [public DPLA](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/), [US Usage Rules](https://www.apple.com/legal/internet-services/itunes/us/terms.html).

An exception for upstream code can be granted only by the relevant rights holders. Owner permission covers the owner's contributions; it cannot relicense the whole upstream core. If the actual terms are compatible, retain the written reasoning and the exact EULA/distribution documents. If they are not, obtain sufficient permission or choose a genuinely compatible implementation/distribution route before shipping. No guessed custom EULA has been filed. [GNU incompatible-library/permission guidance](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#GPLIncompatibleLibs).

## Content, naming and import scope

Apple's rules expressly allow retro-emulator apps, while retaining content, privacy, age and IP obligations. This clears the category question, not EmuDen's particular rights. The local-only candidate avoids relying on an unreviewed public character gallery. Hosting the same images elsewhere or calling them public would not establish permission. User-selected private images are distinct from developer-supplied/promoted content. [Apple4.7 and5.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/).

Bounded name searches identified an existing EMUDEN electrical-component company; that alone neither proves a software conflict nor clears the name. No competing exact-name emulator was confirmed in the searches. The official dynamic USPTO interface yielded no readable records in the available web tool, so no federal-search or similar-mark clearance is claimed. Related goods/services and common-law use matter as well as spelling. A relevant name/presentation assessment remains open. [USPTO clearance guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks), [existing company's own site](https://emuden.com/en/company/network.html).

The remaining AES/NCCH/system-resource import behavior needs an actual US copyright/anti-circumvention assessment, separate from export self-classification. Section1201(a)(2) addresses certain circumvention technologies and section1201(f) has a conditional interoperability provision; neither is a blanket ruling about this implementation. The intended inputs are authorized decrypted software, homebrew and authorized console resources; no ROM/key-download service is offered. Owning a physical game is not a blanket license to download another copy. The copyright/anti-circumvention question is not decided merely by the absence of bundled keys. [Copyright Office digital-files guidance](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-digital.html), [17USC1201 statutory text,2024 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title17/html/USCODE-2024-title17-chap12-sec1201.htm). Current amendments/exemption scope must be checked in the actual legal assessment; this packet does not invent an emulator-wide exemption.

## Concrete external decisions

1. Assess GPL3 sections6/10 (plus independently licensed resource data) against the actual US Apple contracts, chosen EULA and signing/delivery arrangement. Identify a compliant route or the specific permission/implementation change needed.
2. Assess the described import/content-crypto functionality and original presentation/name using the actual22 source and evidence. Separate a confirmed issue from an unexamined risk; do not assume emulation itself is prohibited.
3. Before any submission, reconcile the exact source delivery, rights/export/privacy/age declarations with the final binary and live account terms. Signed-device/regional validation and Apple's review remain separate operational gates, currently paused with other non-legal work.

The prepared evidence makes these decisions reviewable. It does not authorize an attorney engagement, outbound message, fee, agreement or app submission. Final status remains not cleared until the distribution decision is supported and the relevant remaining questions are resolved.
